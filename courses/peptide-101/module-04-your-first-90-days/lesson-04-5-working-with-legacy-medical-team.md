# Lesson 4.5: Working with the Legacy Medical Team — How to Get the Most from Your Check-Ins

**Module**: Module 4 — Your First 90 Days
**Estimated Duration**: 11 minutes
**Learning Objective**: By the end of this lesson you will know how to prepare for and make the most of every Legacy medical team interaction — so your protocol stays precisely calibrated and your team can make the best decisions on your behalf.

---

## HOOK

A medical team check-in is only as good as the information the client brings to it.

Your Legacy providers are skilled clinicians. But they're working with you for a defined window each week — and the difference between a check-in that produces a meaningful protocol adjustment and one that produces "keep doing what you're doing" is almost entirely the quality of the information you bring.

This lesson is about becoming the kind of client whose outcomes reflect the full power of the Legacy Performance model — because you've learned how to use it.

---

## CORE CONTENT

### Key Point 1: What Your Medical Team Is Monitoring — And Why It Matters to Tell Them Everything

Your Legacy licensed medical team is overseeing your protocol from a clinical perspective. At each check-in, they're assessing:

- **Efficacy signals**: Is the protocol producing the expected changes? Are appetite, energy, recovery, or inflammatory markers responding appropriately?
- **Safety monitoring**: Any symptoms that suggest the current dose is inappropriate — too high, too low, or producing unexpected responses?
- **Dose adjustment decisions**: Should the titration continue on schedule? Should it slow down or pause based on your tolerance and response?
- **Lifestyle integration**: Are your nutrition and training behaviors supporting the protocol? Where is the gap?

To make good decisions on all four fronts, they need accurate information. Your job is to provide it — candidly and completely. There is no symptom too small to mention and no lifestyle behavior too embarrassing to disclose. A team that has accurate data can always optimize. A team working with incomplete information makes less accurate decisions.

### Key Point 2: How to Prepare for Your Weekly Check-In

Five minutes of preparation before each check-in makes it dramatically more valuable:

**Review your tracking log**: What were your weekly averages for hunger, energy, and sleep? Did any specific days show unusual patterns?

**Note your injection timing and adherence**: Did you inject on schedule? If you missed a day, when? Timing consistency affects the data your team is interpreting.

**List specific symptoms**: Not "I felt a bit off sometimes" but "I had nausea on Tuesday and Thursday, approximately 3 hours after injection, lasting about 2 hours. It was a 4 out of 10 in severity. It was worse when I didn't eat before injecting."

**Write down your questions**: If you read something about your peptides, had a concern about a symptom, or want to understand a dosing decision, write it down before the check-in. Questions you don't bring to a check-in often don't get answered.

**Report your measurements**: Weekly weight, waist circumference, and any relevant performance changes (new PRs, improved recovery time, reduced injury pain).

### Key Point 3: Questions Worth Asking Your Medical Team

The right questions get you information that makes you a more effective participant in your own protocol. Here's a useful set by program track:

**For any track**:
- "Is my current response within the expected range for this stage of the protocol?"
- "What symptoms should make me contact you immediately vs. report at next check-in?"
- "Is my current titration schedule on track, and what determines whether we adjust it?"

**Fat Loss Track**:
- "Should I request specific lab markers given how I'm responding?"
- "What's the target dose I'm working toward, and what's the timeline?"
- "Is there anything in my nutrition or training that you'd adjust based on my current response?"

**Performance Track**:
- "What IGF-1 level should I be targeting, and when should I test?"
- "Is my injection timing optimized relative to my training schedule?"
- "Are there signs that my GH pulse is responding as expected?"

**Longevity Track**:
- "Which of my injuries should I expect to respond first and fastest?"
- "Should I request a CRP test to track inflammation reduction?"
- "Is there anything about my training load that I should adjust given my current healing priorities?"

### Key Point 4: Between Check-Ins — When to Reach Out Immediately

The weekly check-in structure assumes a stable protocol. But certain situations warrant immediate contact with your Legacy medical team — don't wait for the next scheduled check-in:

**Contact immediately if**:
- Nausea is severe enough that you cannot keep food or water down for more than 24 hours
- You experience severe abdominal pain (not just mild nausea) — this is a priority symptom for anyone on GLP-1 agonists
- An injection site develops spreading redness, warmth, swelling, or discharge that is increasing rather than resolving
- You experience significant heart rate changes, shortness of breath, or chest discomfort
- You have any symptom that feels disproportionate to what was described as expected at your initial protocol orientation

**It's always okay to reach out between check-ins for**:
- A question about whether a specific symptom is concerning
- Clarification on a dosing instruction
- Reporting a significant life change (new medication, illness, surgery) that might affect your protocol
- A positive update you want on record before your next scheduled check-in

Your Legacy team is a resource, not just a scheduled event. Using them proactively between check-ins when genuinely relevant is appropriate — it's what the oversight model is designed for.

## THE AHA MOMENT

The Legacy Performance model is a medically supervised protocol — not a mail-order supplement subscription. The oversight infrastructure exists precisely so your protocol can be adjusted in real time based on your actual biology's response. An informed, data-armed, engaged client gets a fundamentally different outcome from that infrastructure than a passive one. You are an active participant in your own protocol.

---

## YOUR ACTION STEP

Before your next Legacy medical team check-in, prepare a one-page summary using this format: (1) Week's weight and waist measurement, (2) Average hunger/energy/sleep scores, (3) Any symptoms — describe them with timing, severity, and duration, (4) Training notes — what you did and how recovery felt, (5) Questions you want answered. Bring this to every check-in going forward.

---

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. Your medical team's ability to optimize your protocol depends entirely on the accuracy and completeness of the information you provide — preparation before each check-in transforms a routine conversation into a precision adjustment session.
2. The right questions for your specific track give you actionable clarity: expected outcomes, timeline, lab markers to track, and lifestyle adjustments that support the mechanism.
3. Between check-ins, contact your team immediately for any symptom that seems disproportionate — the oversight model exists precisely so problems can be caught and adjusted before they compound.
