# Lesson 4.1: Weeks 1–4 — What's Normal, What's Working, What to Watch

**Module**: Module 4 — Your First 90 Days
**Estimated Duration**: 15 minutes
**Learning Objective**: By the end of this lesson you will know what to expect in the first month of your protocol — adjustment symptoms vs. true side effects, early signals of efficacy, and how to set yourself up for the best possible 90-day result.

---

## HOOK

The first four weeks of any peptide protocol are a unique phase — not because nothing is happening, but because a lot is happening internally before anything shows up externally.

This is the phase where people make the most mistakes: abandoning the protocol because they expected visible results in week one, or panicking about adjustment symptoms that are normal and temporary. This lesson covers everything you need to know to navigate the first month with confidence.

---

## CORE CONTENT

### Key Point 1: The Titration Phase — Why You Start Low

Virtually every Legacy Performance protocol begins at the lowest therapeutic dose and increases gradually. This is called titration, and it's not a gentle introduction before the "real" dose — it is the real strategy.

Here's why:

**Receptor sensitivity**: Starting at a high dose and maintaining it long-term causes receptor downregulation — cells reduce the number of receptors available, blunting the compound's effect over time. Starting low and titrating up maintains receptor sensitivity and ensures the compound's effectiveness doesn't wane.

**Adjustment symptom management**: Every compound that produces significant physiological change produces adjustment symptoms during the early phase. For GLP-1 agonists (tirzepatide, retatrutide), this means nausea, reduced appetite, and possible digestive changes. For GH secretagogues (tesamorelin, ipamorelin), this may mean water retention, mild joint aches, or fatigue in the first two weeks. These are dose-dependent — they're significantly worse if you start high.

**Individual response variation**: No two people metabolize or respond to peptides identically. Titration gives your medical team (and you) data on how your specific biology is responding, so adjustments can be made before problems escalate.

### Key Point 2: Week-by-Week Expectations by Protocol

**Fat Loss Track (Tirzepatide or Retatrutide)**:

*Week 1*: Appetite changes may begin — some people notice reduced interest in food at their first or second meal of the day. Nausea may appear 2–4 hours after injection, lasting 1–4 hours. This is the most common early adjustment symptom.
*Week 2*: Appetite suppression becomes more consistent. If nausea was present, it often begins to ease. Energy may feel slightly lower as your body adapts to reduced caloric intake.
*Week 3*: Most clients notice their relationship with food has shifted. Portions that previously seemed adequate now produce genuine fullness. The urge to snack between meals decreases. Weight loss is typically 2–5 lbs at this point — real but modest.
*Week 4*: Titration to the next dose level (if your medical team has scheduled it). Body weight reduction becomes more apparent. Energy improves as blood sugar stabilizes. Many clients describe this as the week it starts to "click."

**Performance Track (Tesamorelin + Ipamorelin + BPC-157)**:

*Week 1*: Most people don't feel dramatic changes. Some experience mild water retention or morning joint stiffness — this is normal as GH levels begin to rise.
*Week 2*: Sleep quality often starts to improve — specifically the depth and restoration quality of sleep. This is frequently the first reported change.
*Week 3*: Training soreness may begin to ease faster than usual. Recovery time between sessions starts to shorten.
*Week 4*: Energy and mood improvements often become apparent. If there's a chronic injury present, early changes in pain levels may appear at this stage.

**Longevity Track (BPC-157 + TB-500 + Glutathione)**:

*Week 1*: Gut-related symptoms (if present) may begin to improve. Some clients with systemic inflammation report feeling "less puffy" or experiencing reduced joint discomfort within days.
*Week 2*: Training recovery starts to shift — particularly for active clients. Soreness resolves faster.
*Week 3–4*: Energy levels improve. Immune function feels more robust. Chronic injury sites may begin to feel structurally different — not just less painful, but more stable.

### Key Point 3: Adjustment Symptoms vs. True Side Effects — How to Tell

This is one of the most important distinctions in early protocol management.

**Adjustment symptoms** (normal, temporary, dose-dependent):
- Nausea (GLP-1 agonists) — typically peaks at 2–4 hours post-injection, resolves within hours, usually resolves within 2–4 weeks of titration
- Mild fatigue (first 1–2 weeks on any new peptide) — as your body adapts to changed signaling
- Minor water retention (GH secretagogues) — the body temporarily holds water as GH levels rise; resolves within 2–3 weeks
- Injection site irritation — mild redness or soreness at the injection site, lasting 24–48 hours
- Looser stools or constipation (GLP-1 agonists) — as gastric emptying changes; typically self-resolving

**Indicators to contact your Legacy medical team**:
- Severe nausea that prevents eating or drinking for more than 24 hours
- Vomiting that doesn't resolve
- Severe abdominal pain (rare, but important — can indicate pancreatitis in susceptible individuals on GLP-1 agonists)
- Injection site reactions that worsen rather than improve: spreading redness, warmth, or pus
- Significant heart rate changes or chest discomfort
- Any symptom that feels disproportionate to what was described as expected

When in doubt, contact your Legacy medical team. That's what the weekly check-in structure is for — this is not a "wait and see" protocol; it's a monitored one.

### Key Point 4: The Three Habits That Determine Your First 30 Days

The peptide protocol does the heavy lifting. But three habits in weeks 1–4 significantly amplify or undermine it:

**Habit 1 — Protein first, always**: Even when your appetite is suppressed and you don't want to eat much, the protein target is non-negotiable. Aim for your target before other macronutrients. If you hit nothing else in a day, hit protein.

**Habit 2 — Hydration**: GLP-1 agonists increase the risk of dehydration (reduced fluid intake from appetite suppression + gastric emptying changes). GH secretagogues temporarily increase water retention but still require adequate intake. Target a minimum of 8–10 cups of water daily — more if you're training.

**Habit 3 — Log your symptoms**: Keep a simple daily log for weeks 1–4. Note: injection time, any symptoms and their timing, hunger level (1–10), energy level (1–10), sleep quality (1–10). This data transforms your weekly check-in from a vague conversation into a precise adjustment discussion. Your medical team can act on data.

## THE AHA MOMENT

The first 4 weeks are not a preview of your results — they're a setup for your results. The titration, the receptor conditioning, the biological adaptation happening underneath the surface during this month is what makes weeks 5–12 so productive. Patience in the foundation phase is not passive — it's strategic.

---

## YOUR ACTION STEP

Start a simple symptom and tracking log today. You can use a notes app, a spreadsheet, or even a paper notebook. Create five columns: Date / Injection time (if injection day) / Symptoms (description and timing) / Hunger 1–10 / Energy 1–10 / Sleep 1–10. Fill it out daily for the first four weeks. Bring it to every weekly check-in.

---

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. Titration is not a gentle warm-up — it's the core strategy for maintaining receptor sensitivity and managing adjustment symptoms. Don't rush to escalate doses ahead of your medical team's schedule.
2. Adjustment symptoms (nausea, fatigue, water retention, injection site soreness) are normal, dose-dependent, and temporary. True side effects that warrant immediate medical team contact are distinct and described clearly in this lesson.
3. Three habits amplify your first 30 days above all others: protein first (hit your target even when not hungry), adequate hydration, and daily symptom logging.
