## CORE CONTENT

### Key Point 1: Peptides Are Short Chains of Amino Acids

You already know amino acids — they're the building blocks of protein. When you eat chicken breast or drink a protein shake, your body breaks the protein down into individual amino acids and then reassembles them into whatever structures it needs: muscle tissue, enzymes, hormones, and yes — peptides.

A peptide is simply a short chain of amino acids linked together. The word "short" is doing important work here:
- 2 amino acids linked = dipeptide
- 3–10 amino acids = oligopeptide
- 10–50 amino acids = peptide
- 50+ amino acids = protein (which is why insulin, at 51 amino acids, is technically a small protein)

The key insight is this: peptides are *natural language* that your cells already speak. Every organ in your body produces and responds to peptides. They are the primary signaling system that tells cells what to do, when to do it, and how much.

### Key Point 2: How Peptides Are Different from Steroids

This is the most important distinction to understand — both for your own peace of mind and for accurate conversations with anyone who asks what you're on.

**Steroids** (anabolic steroids like testosterone, trenbolone, or synthetic derivatives) are fat-soluble molecules derived from cholesterol. They enter cells directly through the cell membrane, travel to the nucleus, and alter gene expression. They're powerful, they work, and they carry well-documented risks: cardiovascular strain, liver stress, hormonal suppression, and shutting down your body's own production of the hormones they replace.

**Peptides** are water-soluble chains of amino acids. They do not enter cells. Instead, they bind to receptors on the cell's surface — like a key fitting into a lock — and trigger a downstream response *from outside the cell*. Your body processes them the same way it processes dietary amino acids: they're broken down and eliminated naturally.

The practical implications:
- Peptides do not shut down your natural hormone production (they can actually stimulate it)
- Peptides are not liver-toxic
- Peptides do not carry the cardiovascular risks associated with anabolic steroids
- Peptides are prescribed by licensed physicians (at Legacy Performance) through licensed pharmacies

### Key Point 3: Peptides vs. Hormones — Related, But Not the Same

Some hormones are peptides (insulin and glucagon are classic examples). But not all peptides are hormones, and not all hormones are peptides (testosterone, cortisol, and estrogen are steroid hormones, not peptides).

The peptides used in your Legacy Performance protocol are primarily *signaling peptides* — they tell your body to produce, release, or up-regulate something it already makes. For example:
- Tesamorelin tells your pituitary gland to release growth hormone — it doesn't replace GH, it prompts your body to produce more of its own.
- Tirzepatide acts like GLP-1 and GIP — hormones your gut already makes — at your receptors, amplifying a signal your body already sends.

This is a fundamentally different mechanism from hormone replacement therapy (HRT), where you're supplying the hormone externally. With most peptides, you're restoring the *signal*, not bypassing the system.
