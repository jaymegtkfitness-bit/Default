## HOOK

Somewhere in your gut right now, a protein is doing something remarkable.

Gastric juice — the acidic fluid that breaks down your food — contains a protective protein that lines and repairs your stomach so it doesn't digest itself. Within that protein is a sequence of 15 amino acids that, when isolated and administered therapeutically, produces some of the most striking healing responses seen in peptide research.

That sequence is BPC-157. Body Protection Compound. Fifteen amino acids. One of the most discussed and studied peptides in sports medicine and longevity research.

---

## CORE CONTENT

### Key Point 1: What BPC-157 Is and Where It Comes From

BPC-157 is a pentadecapeptide — a chain of exactly 15 amino acids — derived from a naturally occurring protein in human gastric juice. It is not a foreign molecule invented in a lab. It is a sequence your body already produces, isolated, synthesized in pharmaceutical-grade form, and administered at therapeutic concentrations.

The "body protection" in its name is not marketing language. It describes the function of the parent protein — protecting the gut lining from self-digestion by gastric acid — and it turns out this protection extends far beyond the gut when the peptide sequence is administered systemically.

BPC-157 is currently classified as a research peptide in the United States (it has not gone through the FDA approval process for any indication), but it has an extensive preclinical research literature — primarily in animal models — covering wound healing, tendon and ligament repair, gut healing, nerve regeneration, and cardiovascular protection. Its safety profile in preclinical models is notably clean.

### Key Point 2: The Primary Mechanisms — How BPC-157 Heals

BPC-157 works through several distinct but interconnected pathways:

**Angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation)**: BPC-157 upregulates VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) — the primary driver of new blood vessel growth. Injured tissue heals slowly partly because blood supply is compromised. BPC-157 accelerates the formation of new vasculature to the injury site, dramatically increasing the delivery of oxygen, nutrients, and immune cells.

**Growth factor receptor upregulation**: BPC-157 increases the expression of receptors for growth factors including EGF (epidermal growth factor) and HGF (hepatocyte growth factor) — effectively making tissues more sensitive to the body's own repair signals.

**Nitric oxide modulation**: BPC-157 has complex effects on the nitric oxide (NO) system, protecting endothelial cells (the lining of blood vessels) and reducing local inflammation while supporting vasodilation in healing tissue.

**Tendon fibroblast stimulation**: Tendons are notoriously slow to heal because they have poor blood supply and a low density of fibroblasts (the cells that produce collagen). BPC-157 has been specifically shown to accelerate tendon fibroblast proliferation and collagen production — which is why it's one of the most discussed compounds for tendon injuries.

**Gut lining protection and repair**: True to its origin, BPC-157 has potent effects on gastrointestinal tissue. It protects against gut lining damage from NSAIDs (like ibuprofen), alcohol, and chronic stress, and accelerates repair of existing gut barrier dysfunction.

### Key Point 3: What BPC-157 Is Used For in the Legacy Programs

BPC-157 appears in two Legacy Performance programs, reflecting its dual role:

**In the Longevity Program** (with TB-500 and glutathione):
The primary focus is gut, tendon, and ligament repair — systemic recovery for members dealing with chronic joint pain, recurring injuries, slow recovery between training sessions, or gut dysfunction. BPC-157 is the targeted repair signal; TB-500 provides the systemic healing substrate; glutathione provides cellular protection from the oxidative stress that chronic injury and inflammation generate.

**In the Performance Program** (with tesamorelin and ipamorelin):
Here, BPC-157 plays an injury prevention and recovery optimization role. High-output athletes — CrossFitters, runners, lifters — are continuously stressing tendons, ligaments, and muscle. BPC-157 runs in the background of the GH-axis stack to keep repair ahead of breakdown, preventing the accumulation of microdamage that eventually becomes an acute injury.

### Key Point 4: Administration — Systemic vs. Local Injection

BPC-157 can be administered in two ways, and the choice depends on the goal:

**Subcutaneous or intramuscular injection (systemic)**: The most common approach for general recovery, gut healing, and non-specific tissue repair. The peptide enters the bloodstream and exerts effects throughout the body. This is the approach used in the Legacy Performance programs.

**Local injection at or near an injury site**: For targeted tendon, ligament, or joint injuries, some practitioners inject BPC-157 directly adjacent to the injury. Preclinical evidence suggests local injection may produce faster and more concentrated repair at the specific site.

Discuss with your Legacy medical team which approach is best for your specific situation. If you have a chronic or active injury, that's a relevant detail to share at your check-in.

**Route matters**: BPC-157 is also sometimes taken orally (in capsule form) for gut-specific effects. Oral BPC-157 survives digestion and reaches the gut lining directly. For systemic effects — including the tendon and muscle benefits — injection is the appropriate route.

## THE AHA MOMENT

BPC-157 doesn't "heal" you the way a drug targets a pathogen or blocks a receptor. It restores the body's own repair capacity — increasing blood supply, upregulating growth factor sensitivity, and stimulating the fibroblasts that produce the collagen your tendons and ligaments are made of. It's like turning up the signal your body already uses to fix itself.
