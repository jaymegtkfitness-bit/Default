## HOOK

If tirzepatide hitting two receptor systems simultaneously was a breakthrough — and it was — then what happens when you add a third?

Retatrutide (LY3437943, from Eli Lilly) is the answer to that question. Phase 2 clinical trial data published in 2023 showed average weight loss of 24.2% of body weight over 48 weeks. Some participants lost over 26% of their body weight — numbers that, a decade ago, were only achievable with bariatric surgery.

But the more important story isn't just *how much* weight is lost. It's *what kind* of fat is lost — and what the glucagon receptor does that neither GLP-1 nor GIP alone can accomplish.

---

## CORE CONTENT

### Key Point 1: The Three Receptors — What the Third One Adds

Retatrutide activates three receptors simultaneously:

**GLP-1 receptor** (same as tirzepatide):
- Slows gastric emptying, reduces appetite via hypothalamic signaling, stimulates glucose-dependent insulin secretion, reduces glucagon from the pancreas

**GIP receptor** (same as tirzepatide):
- Amplifies insulin secretion synergistically, shifts fat cells from storage to utilization, reduces fat tissue inflammation

**Glucagon receptor** (the new addition):
- This is where retatrutide separates itself from tirzepatide

Glucagon is often thought of as the "anti-insulin" — it raises blood sugar by signaling the liver to release stored glucose. But activating glucagon receptors in the context of a GLP-1/GIP agonist produces a different, more nuanced effect:

- **Increased energy expenditure**: Glucagon receptor activation increases thermogenesis — your body burns more calories at rest. This is why retatrutide produces greater total weight loss on similar caloric restriction.
- **Visceral fat mobilization**: Glucagon is particularly effective at mobilizing visceral adipose tissue — the dangerous fat stored around your organs (belly fat). This fat is metabolically active in harmful ways: it drives insulin resistance, systemic inflammation, and cardiovascular risk. GLP-1 and GIP help, but the glucagon receptor is the primary signal for mobilizing this specific fat depot.
- **Hepatic fat reduction**: The liver benefits significantly from glucagon receptor activation — fatty liver (hepatic steatosis) improves more aggressively with the triple agonist than with dual agonists.

### Key Point 2: Dosing Structure — How Retatrutide Is Used at Legacy Performance

The Legacy Performance Retatrutide Protocol uses a titration approach similar to tirzepatide:

- **Starting dose**: 1mg weekly (lowest dose to establish tolerance)
- **Titration**: Typically progressing toward 2mg, then 4mg, then up to 8–12mg based on response and tolerability
- **Timeline**: Titration occurs over the 90-day program period, with your medical team guiding progression based on your weekly check-in data

Retatrutide is dosed subcutaneously once weekly, like tirzepatide. The key difference in the titration experience: glucagon receptor activation can produce slightly more pronounced nausea in early titration phases, which is why starting very conservatively at 1mg is important.

The protocol also includes Lipo-B MIC injections — which support hepatic fat processing and provide the methyl donors and B vitamins that energy metabolism requires during aggressive fat mobilization.

### Key Point 3: Retatrutide vs. Tirzepatide — How to Think About Which Protocol Is Right for You

This is a decision your Legacy medical team will guide based on your health history and goals. But here's the framework for understanding the difference:

**Tirzepatide (Option 1) is the right choice when**:
- This is your first experience with GLP-1 class compounds
- Your primary goal is fat loss with strong appetite management and insulin sensitivity improvement
- You want a well-characterized compound with extensive Phase 3 and real-world data

**Retatrutide (Option 2) may be the better choice when**:
- You want maximum fat loss potential, particularly from visceral/abdominal fat
- You have elevated liver fat (hepatic steatosis) or significantly impaired metabolic health
- You've used a GLP-1-only agent previously and want to escalate the mechanism
- Your medical team identifies that the thermogenic (calorie-burning) component would be particularly beneficial for your metabolism

Both protocols are priced identically at Legacy Performance ($2,495/90 days) and include the same clinical support infrastructure. The choice is about matching the mechanism to your biology — not about which is "better."

### Key Point 4: The Muscle Preservation Priority — Even More Important with Retatrutide

The aggressive fat loss that retatrutide drives can, if unmanaged, include some lean mass loss. This is a documented phenomenon with all GLP-1 class drugs — rapid weight loss from any source can include muscle.

At the doses used in retatrutide trials, studies showed approximately 10–15% of total weight lost was lean mass — compared to roughly 25–30% lean mass loss in typical caloric restriction without pharmacological support. So retatrutide is better than diet alone at preserving muscle. But it's not perfect.

The three strategies that protect muscle during your retatrutide protocol:
1. **High protein intake**: 0.7–1g per pound of target body weight daily. Non-negotiable.
2. **Resistance training**: At least 3 days per week. Muscle needs the anabolic stimulus to justify preservation.
3. **Adequate calories**: Don't use the appetite suppression as license to eat 800 calories per day. The Legacy Personalized Nutrition Program will calculate a sustainable deficit — not a starvation response.
