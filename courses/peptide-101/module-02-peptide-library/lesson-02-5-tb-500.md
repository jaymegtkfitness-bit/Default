## CORE CONTENT

### Key Point 1: What Thymosin Beta-4 Is and Why the Fragment Matters

Thymosin beta-4 is a 43-amino-acid protein found in virtually all human cells, with especially high concentrations in platelets (which rush to injury sites first) and immune cells. It was first identified in the thymus gland — the organ that trains immune cells — but subsequent research found it throughout the body.

Its primary cellular functions:
- **Actin regulation**: Thymosin beta-4 sequesters G-actin (the building block of actin filaments) inside cells. Actin is the protein that makes up the cytoskeleton — the structural scaffolding inside every cell. When a cell needs to move (as it does during wound healing and repair), thymosin beta-4 releases actin, allowing the cytoskeleton to reorganize and the cell to migrate.
- **Cell migration**: In wound healing, repair cells need to move into damaged tissue. Thymosin beta-4 is one of the primary mediators of this migration — it's essentially the signal that says "repair cells, move here."
- **Anti-inflammatory signaling**: Thymosin beta-4 downregulates NF-κB — one of the master transcription factors that drives inflammatory gene expression. This is how it reduces chronic inflammation without suppressing the acute inflammation needed for proper healing.

TB-500 is the 17-amino-acid fragment of thymosin beta-4 that contains the primary active region responsible for actin binding and the downstream healing effects. It is smaller than the full protein, which means it's more bioavailable and more practical to administer.

### Key Point 3: What TB-500 Does in the Body — The Clinical Picture

Research on TB-500 (and its parent molecule thymosin beta-4) covers several systems:

**Muscle repair**: Studies show acceleration of satellite cell activation — the muscle stem cells responsible for repairing damage after exercise. This translates to faster recovery and reduced DOMS in actively training individuals.

**Cardiovascular tissue**: Thymosin beta-4 has been studied in cardiac injury models — it promotes cardiomyocyte survival and supports repair after ischemia (reduced blood flow). While this research is primarily preclinical, it's relevant for athletes and older adults whose cardiovascular tissue experiences stress.

**Nerve tissue**: TB-500 appears to support nerve repair and axon regeneration in injury models — particularly relevant for people with nerve-related pain (sciatica, neuropathy from chronic inflammation) who are on the Longevity Program.

**Skin and wound healing**: Some of the strongest clinical data for thymosin beta-4 comes from wound healing studies — it significantly accelerates the closure of chronic wounds and promotes tissue remodeling.

**Systemic inflammation reduction**: The NF-κB inhibition mechanism means TB-500 is working at the genetic transcription level to reduce the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines throughout the body — not just at an injury site. For anyone with elevated CRP or systemic chronic inflammation, this is a significant benefit.

TB-500 in the Legacy Performance Longevity Program is administered subcutaneously (under the skin, not into muscle or joints) by injection. Standard protocols typically involve:
