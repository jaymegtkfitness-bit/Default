## HOOK

Every cell in your body is under oxidative attack.

This isn't a dramatic statement — it's basic biochemistry. Oxygen, the molecule that keeps you alive, also produces byproducts of its own metabolism called reactive oxygen species (ROS) — what most people call "free radicals." These molecules damage cell membranes, proteins, and DNA. Left unchecked, they accelerate aging, drive chronic disease, and compromise recovery from exercise and injury.

Your body has an entire defense system against this damage. At the center of it is a tripeptide made of three amino acids — glutamate, cysteine, and glycine — called glutathione. And it may be the most underappreciated molecule in the human body.

---

## CORE CONTENT

### Key Point 1: What Glutathione Is and Why It's "Master"

Glutathione (GSH) is a tripeptide — technically making it the simplest type of peptide, a chain of just three amino acids. Your body produces it in virtually every cell, with the highest concentrations in the liver (which faces the greatest detoxification burden), lungs, and red blood cells.

It's called the "master antioxidant" for two reasons:

**First**: It directly neutralizes reactive oxygen species. Glutathione donates an electron to a free radical, stabilizing it and preventing damage. In the process, glutathione becomes oxidized itself (GSSG — glutathione disulfide) — but unlike most antioxidants, it can be recycled back to its active form (GSH) by an enzyme called glutathione reductase. This recyclability is what makes it a high-capacity, sustainable defense system rather than a one-time use molecule.

**Second**: It regenerates other antioxidants. Vitamin C and vitamin E become oxidized when they neutralize free radicals. Glutathione recycles both back to their active forms — amplifying the antioxidant capacity of your entire defense network. Without adequate glutathione, your other antioxidants are depleted faster than they can be replaced.

### Key Point 2: What Glutathione Does Beyond Antioxidant Defense

While its antioxidant function is foundational, glutathione has several other critical roles:

**Liver detoxification**: Phase II liver detoxification — the process that conjugates toxins, metabolic byproducts, and drugs for elimination — is heavily glutathione-dependent. Glutathione binds to toxins directly (glutathione conjugation) and makes them water-soluble for excretion. People with high toxic burdens, medication use, or alcohol consumption deplete glutathione fastest.

**Immune function**: Glutathione is essential for proper T-cell proliferation and immune cell activation. Immune deficiency in conditions ranging from HIV to chronic infection is partially mediated by glutathione depletion. Replenishing glutathione supports immune balance — not just immune activation.

**Mitochondrial protection**: Mitochondria — the energy-producing organelles inside each cell — are under constant oxidative stress because they're the primary site of oxygen metabolism. Glutathione within the mitochondria (mitochondrial GSH) is specifically responsible for protecting these organelles from the very process they use to make energy. Adequate mitochondrial glutathione is a prerequisite for sustained energy production.

**Inflammation regulation**: Glutathione modulates NF-κB activity (the same inflammatory transcription factor TB-500 targets) — meaning it reduces the genetic expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Low glutathione is associated with elevated chronic inflammation across multiple conditions.

### Key Point 3: Why Glutathione Levels Decline — And Who Is Most at Risk

Oral glutathione is poorly absorbed — the digestive process breaks the tripeptide into its component amino acids before it reaches systemic circulation. This is why injectable or IV glutathione has become the standard approach in functional medicine and why Legacy Performance includes it in its Longevity Protocol.

Glutathione levels decline with:
- **Age**: Synthesis slows and recycling efficiency decreases
- **Intense exercise**: Training produces significant oxidative stress — athletes have higher glutathione turnover
- **Chronic stress**: Psychological stress depletes glutathione through sustained cortisol and catecholamine production
- **Poor diet**: Adequate cysteine (the rate-limiting amino acid for glutathione synthesis) requires sufficient dietary protein and sulfur-rich foods
- **Alcohol**: Acetaldehyde (the primary toxic metabolite of alcohol) depletes glutathione significantly
- **Environmental toxins**: Heavy metal exposure, pollution, and pesticide burden all increase detoxification demand

For anyone on the Longevity Program — particularly those in their 30s or beyond who train hard, face chronic stress, or have any metabolic dysfunction — glutathione is likely below optimal, even if blood levels fall within "normal" ranges.

### Key Point 4: How Injectable Glutathione Works — and What to Expect

Injectable glutathione delivers the reduced, active form (GSH) directly into systemic circulation, bypassing the digestive degradation that makes oral supplementation largely ineffective.

**What you may notice during your Longevity Protocol**:
- **Skin clarity**: Glutathione inhibits the enzyme tyrosinase, which drives melanin (skin pigment) production. Brightening and evening of skin tone is a commonly reported effect — not an intended primary outcome, but a visible signal that cellular oxidative burden is decreasing.
- **Energy improvement**: As mitochondrial glutathione rises and cellular oxidative stress falls, many people report more consistent, sustained energy — particularly in the afternoon.
- **Faster post-training recovery**: The reduction in oxidative stress that follows hard training resolves more quickly.
- **Reduced systemic inflammation**: If you had elevated CRP or other inflammatory markers, these tend to trend downward over 6–12 weeks.

**Important interaction**: Vitamin C taken alongside glutathione administration helps maintain glutathione's active form — consider discussing with your Legacy medical team whether supplemental vitamin C is appropriate alongside your protocol.
