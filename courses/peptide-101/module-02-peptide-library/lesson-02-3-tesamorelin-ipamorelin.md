## HOOK

Here's something most people over 35 don't realize: the decline in their body composition, recovery speed, sleep quality, and energy is not primarily driven by testosterone. It's driven by growth hormone.

GH production peaks in your late teens and early 20s. By 40, most people are producing about half the GH they had at their peak. By 50, it's closer to a quarter. And unlike testosterone — which has an entire medical specialty devoted to replacement — GH decline is widely accepted as "just aging."

Tesamorelin and ipamorelin are the answer to that resignation.

## CORE CONTENT

### Key Point 1: Why GH Declines — And Why It Matters More Than Most People Think

Growth hormone does not act like most people expect it to. It's not just about building muscle. Here's what GH governs across your body:

- **Body composition**: GH promotes fat oxidation and lean tissue maintenance — particularly the fat-to-muscle ratio. This is why GH-deficient adults tend toward central adiposity (belly fat) and sarcopenia (muscle loss) even with adequate diet and exercise.
- **Recovery speed**: GH is the primary orchestrator of cellular repair. It signals connective tissue, muscle, bone, and organ repair after stress. When GH is low, recovery from training — and from daily life — slows dramatically.
- **Sleep quality**: The largest pulse of GH release occurs within the first hour of deep sleep. Low GH is associated with reduced slow-wave sleep — and reduced slow-wave sleep further impairs GH release. It's a downward spiral.
- **Cognitive function**: GH receptors are present in the brain. GH supports neuronal health, working memory, and mood regulation. GH-deficient adults commonly report brain fog and low mood.
- **Skin and connective tissue**: Collagen synthesis is GH-dependent. Declining GH contributes to thinning skin, joint stiffness, and reduced tendon resilience.
- **Metabolic rate**: GH is anti-insulin in its direct action — it promotes fat burning and limits fat storage. As GH declines, metabolic rate decreases independent of muscle mass changes.

### Key Point 2: Tesamorelin — Restoring the GHRH Signal

Tesamorelin is a synthetic analogue of GHRH — the growth hormone-releasing hormone produced by your hypothalamus. It's the most upstream intervention in the GH axis: rather than injecting growth hormone directly (which suppresses your pituitary's own function), tesamorelin tells your pituitary to release more of its own GH.

This distinction is critical:
- **Exogenous GH injection** bypasses your hypothalamic-pituitary axis and, over time, causes the pituitary to downregulate its own GH production. You become dependent.
- **Tesamorelin** stimulates the pituitary to produce GH on its own schedule, in pulses, preserving the natural regulatory feedback loops. When you stop, your baseline function returns to what it was before the protocol — you haven't suppressed anything.

Tesamorelin has the strongest clinical evidence of any GHRH analogue for reducing visceral fat. It was FDA-approved specifically for HIV-associated lipodystrophy (abnormal fat distribution) — a condition with extensive visceral fat accumulation — based on trials showing 15–20% reductions in visceral fat area with preserved lean mass.

In the Legacy Performance Performance Program, tesamorelin's role is:
- Drive the primary GH-releasing signal
- Reduce visceral fat independently of caloric restriction
- Improve IGF-1 levels (the downstream marker of GH activity)
- Support connective tissue health and recovery

### Key Point 3: Ipamorelin — The Clean GHRP That Amplifies the Signal

Ipamorelin is a growth hormone-releasing peptide (GHRP) — specifically, a ghrelin receptor agonist. While tesamorelin acts at the hypothalamus to trigger GHRH release, ipamorelin acts directly at the pituitary's ghrelin receptors to stimulate additional GH release.

The result: tesamorelin sends the signal from the hypothalamus; ipamorelin amplifies it at the pituitary. Together, they produce a significantly larger GH pulse than either would alone.

What makes ipamorelin stand out among GHRPs:
- **Selective GH release**: Older GHRPs (GHRP-2, GHRP-6) also stimulate cortisol and prolactin alongside GH — stress hormones you don't want elevated. Ipamorelin is highly selective for GH release without meaningful cortisol or prolactin elevation.
- **No hunger spike**: GHRP-6 famously causes intense hunger (ghrelin is the "hunger hormone"). Ipamorelin produces minimal to no hunger increase — critical for clients not on a fat loss protocol who don't need appetite complications.
- **Pulsatile, physiological release**: Ipamorelin produces a natural GH pulse — rising and falling in a pattern that mimics endogenous GH release rather than creating a flat, artificially sustained elevation.

### Key Point 4: The Stack in Practice — Timing, Dosing, and What to Expect

**Dosing approach in the Legacy Performance Performance Program**:

**Timeline of effects**:
- **Weeks 1–3**: Most people notice improved sleep quality first — deeper, more restorative sleep. This is often the first signal that the GH axis is responding.
- **Weeks 3–6**: Recovery between training sessions begins to accelerate. Soreness resolves faster. The "two-day delay" of DOMS (delayed onset muscle soreness) shortens.

- **Weeks 10–12**: IGF-1 levels (if tracked with labs) should show meaningful improvement. Energy, cognitive clarity, and mood are commonly reported as improved.

**What to track**:
- IGF-1 blood level (request from your Legacy medical team or bring your baseline from recent labs)
- Waist circumference (visceral fat reduction is most visible here)
- Training recovery time — a subjective but powerful indicator
- Sleep quality (a simple 1–10 nightly rating tracked in your notes)
