# Lesson 6.3: When to Reassess and How to Evolve Your Protocol

**Module**: Module 6 — Your Long-Term Peptide Blueprint
**Estimated Duration**: 13 minutes
**Learning Objective**: By the end of this lesson you will know the specific triggers that indicate it's time to reassess your protocol, the framework for making that reassessment productive, and how to evolve your program as your biology and goals change over time.

---

## HOOK

Health optimization is not a destination. It's a moving target — because your biology changes, your life changes, and the goals that motivated you when you started your first protocol are likely different from the goals that will motivate you at year two or year three.

The most successful Legacy Performance clients aren't the ones who found a protocol and stuck with it indefinitely. They're the ones who built a system for reassessment — regularly asking "is this still the right approach for where I am now?" — and adjusting strategically when the answer is no.

This lesson gives you the framework for that ongoing question.

---

## CORE CONTENT

### Key Point 1: Scheduled Reassessment Points — The Built-In Calendar

Reassessment should not wait for things to go wrong. Proactive reassessment at predetermined points keeps your protocol ahead of your biology's evolution rather than chasing it.

**90-Day Reassessment** (end of each protocol cycle):
This is the most important reassessment point. At 90 days:
- Compare all tracked metrics to baseline (weight, waist, lab markers, subjective ratings)
- Review which goals have been met, which are partially met, and which remain unchanged
- Discuss with your Legacy medical team: continue the same track, evolve the dose/frequency, or transition to a new track
- Order advanced biomarkers if not done mid-cycle

**6-Month Review**:
A broader strategic review. After two 90-day cycles:
- What has changed about your body composition, metabolic health, and functional capacity?
- Do your goals remain the same, or have they evolved (e.g., initial goal was fat loss; now that's achieved, performance optimization is the primary motivation)?
- Is there new evidence or new compounds relevant to your biology that warrant consideration?
- If you haven't done the Genetic Blueprint Protocol, is now the right time?

**Annual Comprehensive Assessment**:
A full biomarker panel, protocol review, and goals alignment with your Legacy medical team. Think of this as your annual health board meeting — a high-altitude review of where you are relative to your long-term trajectory.

### Key Point 2: Unscheduled Triggers That Indicate Immediate Reassessment

Between scheduled reviews, certain events or patterns should trigger a protocol reassessment conversation with your Legacy medical team:

**Body changes**:
- Weight regain of more than 5% without obvious caloric explanation
- Loss of lean mass that was previously preserved
- Return of symptoms that the protocol had resolved (fatigue, joint pain, appetite dysregulation)

**Lab changes**:
- IGF-1 declining below optimal range despite continued Performance Track protocol
- CRP rising again after having improved
- Insulin or glucose markers trending in the wrong direction

**Life changes**:
- Significant increase in chronic psychological stress (new job, major personal disruption)
- Starting a new medication — some medications interact with peptide mechanisms or affect metabolism
- Pregnancy planning (GLP-1 agonists are contraindicated in pregnancy; GH secretagogues should be discussed with an OB in this context)
- Significant change in training volume or type
- Major dietary change or restriction

**Goal evolution**:
- Your primary goal has been achieved and a new priority has emerged
- You've read about a protocol option (stacking, new compound) you want to discuss
- Your performance benchmarks or body composition targets have been revised

### Key Point 3: The Framework for a Productive Reassessment Conversation

A protocol reassessment is most productive when you arrive with data and a clear articulation of your current state and future goals. Use this structure:

**1. Current state summary** (what the data shows):
- My 90-day results across tracked metrics
- Where I am relative to my starting point
- Current biomarker picture
- How my lifestyle foundations are performing (sleep, stress, training, nutrition)

**2. Goal alignment check**:
- Is my primary goal the same as when I started?
- What's my single biggest remaining gap — the outcome I want most that I don't yet have?
- Has anything about my health picture changed that should affect protocol priorities?

**3. Protocol performance assessment**:
- Is my current protocol still producing a response? Or has adaptation occurred?
- Are there specific elements of the protocol that I haven't been adherent to that might explain suboptimal outcomes?
- Are there elements that are clearly working that should be maintained?

**4. Evolution options** (discuss with your team):
- Continue current protocol with dose adjustment
- Transition to a new primary track
- Layer a second track onto the existing protocol
- Take a protocol off-period before re-initiating

### Key Point 4: How Your Protocol Evolves Across Life Stages

Your biology at 35 is different from 45 is different from 55. The peptides don't change — their mechanisms are fixed. But which mechanisms your body needs most changes as you move through life stages:

**35–40**: Often the first significant experience of GH decline, metabolic slowdown, and recovery lag. This is typically when the Performance Track produces the most dramatic felt improvement — restoring what has recently been lost.

**40–50**: Metabolic dysfunction often becomes more prominent. Many clients add or shift toward the Fat Loss Track in this decade — visceral fat accumulation accelerates as insulin sensitivity declines and GH output further falls. Genetic Blueprint Protocol becomes increasingly valuable for root-cause precision.

**50+**: The Longevity Track often becomes the foundation — the body's repair capacity declines significantly, and accumulation of structural damage from decades of training and stress makes BPC-157/TB-500/Glutathione support essential. Performance and Fat Loss protocols continue on top of this foundation.

This is a generalization, not a prescription — your genetic profile and individual biology may create different priorities. But understanding the developmental arc of how health optimization needs evolve helps contextualize why the Legacy Performance model emphasizes ongoing protocol evolution rather than a fixed approach.

## THE AHA MOMENT

A protocol that was optimal for you six months ago may not be optimal for you today — not because it failed, but because it worked. Successful outcomes change the biology, which changes what's needed next. The ability to identify when to evolve — not just stick with what worked — is the distinguishing skill of long-term health optimization.

---

## YOUR ACTION STEP

Before your final Legacy medical team check-in of your current protocol cycle, write out your protocol evolution framework: (1) What were my goals when I started? (2) Which have been achieved? (3) What is my single biggest remaining gap? (4) What does the next protocol cycle need to address to close that gap? Bring this one-page summary to your closing check-in. It will produce a more strategic, forward-looking conversation than any other preparation you could do.

---

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. Proactive reassessment at 90 days, 6 months, and annually — rather than waiting for things to go wrong — keeps your protocol aligned with your evolving biology and goals.
2. Unscheduled reassessment triggers include weight or lab marker regression, goal evolution, new medications, significant life changes, and plateau despite protocol adherence.
3. As you move through life stages, the priority balance between Fat Loss (metabolic), Performance (GH axis), and Longevity (repair) shifts — protocol evolution is not failure, it's precision.
