# Lesson 6.1: Cycle vs. Continuous Protocols — What the Research Shows

**Module**: Module 6 — Your Long-Term Peptide Blueprint

## HOOK

One of the most frequently asked questions after a successful 90-day protocol: do I stop now, or do I keep going?

And underneath that question is a legitimate concern: if I keep using this compound continuously, will my body adapt, become dependent, or stop responding? Will I get diminishing returns?

The answer is different for each compound in the Legacy stack — and understanding why gives you the framework for a long-term strategy that doesn't leave you guessing.

---

## CORE CONTENT

### Key Point 1: The Compounds That Benefit from Cycling vs. Those That Don't

Not all peptides have the same relationship with continuous use:

**Compounds where cycling is advisable**:

*GH secretagogues (Tesamorelin + Ipamorelin)*: The hypothalamic-pituitary axis has natural regulatory rhythms. Continuous, uninterrupted stimulation with GHRH analogues over very long periods may reduce pituitary responsiveness to the signal. Standard practice is to run 12–16 week protocols followed by 4–8 week off-periods to allow the axis to reset and maintain sensitivity. During the off-period, natural GH pulsatility — restored and improved by the protocol — continues at its new elevated baseline.

*BPC-157 (for specific injury repair targets)*: Once a structural injury has been repaired (typically 12–16 weeks), continuous BPC-157 use for that injury is not necessary. BPC-157 can then be used intermittently as needed (during high-training-load periods, new injury events, or seasonal maintenance) rather than as a continuous background protocol.

**Compounds where continuous use is supported**:

*Tirzepatide and Retatrutide*: The clinical trial data and FDA approval model for these compounds supports long-term continuous use. The metabolic conditions they address (insulin resistance, obesity, metabolic dysfunction) are chronic conditions. Stopping tirzepatide in the SURMOUNT trials led to weight regain in a significant percentage of participants — reflecting that the metabolic dysfunction that made weight loss difficult has not been cured, only managed. For clients with significant underlying metabolic dysfunction, continuous or long-term cyclical use is medically appropriate and consistent with the evidence base.

*Glutathione*: Antioxidant demand is continuous, not episodic. If your lifestyle (training, stress, aging) continues to deplete glutathione faster than your body synthesizes it, the logic for ongoing injectable support remains. This is typically reassessed based on symptoms, lab markers, and lifestyle changes rather than a fixed cycling schedule.

### Key Point 2: What Happens When You Stop — Compound by Compound

**Stopping Tirzepatide or Retatrutide**:
GLP-1 receptor agonist effects are not permanent. When you stop, your natural GLP-1/GIP signaling returns to its pre-treatment level — which, in metabolically dysfunctional individuals, is what allowed the weight gain in the first place. This is not a side effect or a failure — it's the nature of managing a chronic condition.

What changes: Appetite gradually returns to pre-treatment levels (over 2–4 weeks). Weight regain occurs in proportion to caloric intake and the metabolic habits formed during the protocol. Clients who used their 90 days to build nutrition habits and lifestyle structure maintain a higher percentage of their results than those who relied entirely on appetite suppression.

**Stopping GH Secretagogues**:
When tesamorelin and ipamorelin are stopped, GH pulsatility and IGF-1 return toward baseline — but often to a higher baseline than pre-protocol, because the improvement in sleep quality, stress management, and metabolic health that the protocol facilitated continues. The off-period is not a return to square one. It's a plateau at a higher level than where you started, from which the next cycle builds.

**Stopping BPC-157 and TB-500**:
Structural changes (healed tendons, repaired gut lining, reduced fibrous scar tissue) persist after stopping. These peptides don't create physiological dependency — they facilitate repair, and the repair is durable. The rationale for returning to these compounds is new injury, training demands that outpace natural recovery, or maintenance of particularly vulnerable tissue.

### Key Point 3: Designing Your Long-Term Protocol Strategy

The most successful long-term Legacy Performance clients approach their health as a multi-cycle project, not a one-time intervention:

**For Fat Loss/Metabolic clients**:
- Cycle 1 (90 days): Full Fat Loss Protocol — significant body weight and metabolic improvement
- Consolidation period (4–8 weeks): Maintain at lower dose or move to maintenance dosing; focus on embedding nutrition habits
- Cycle 2 option A: Repeat Fat Loss Protocol if additional weight reduction is needed
- Cycle 2 option B: Transition to Performance Protocol now that metabolic foundation is established
- Long-term: Ongoing maintenance at a lower dose or seasonal cycling based on goals and medical team assessment

**For Performance clients**:
- Cycle 1 (90 days): Full Performance Protocol — GH axis restoration, body composition, recovery
- Off-period (4–6 weeks): No GH secretagogues; maintain training and nutrition; natural GH pulsatility continues at elevated baseline
- Cycle 2: Repeat Performance Protocol or evolve to Fat Loss + Performance combination
- Annual reassessment with IGF-1 labs to confirm maintained optimization

**For Longevity clients**:
- Cycle 1 (90 days): Full Longevity Protocol — acute injury repair, systemic inflammation resolution
- Post-protocol assessment: Which structural issues resolved? What remains?
- Long-term: Seasonal maintenance cycles (BPC-157 + TB-500 for 4–6 weeks during high training load periods or at onset of new injury signals); glutathione continued based on ongoing oxidative stress burden

### Key Point 4: The Role of Your Medical Team in Long-Term Strategy

Long-term protocol design requires ongoing medical oversight for the same reasons your initial protocol did: dosing decisions, monitoring for efficacy and safety, and clinical adjustment based on changing biomarkers and life circumstances.

Your Legacy Performance membership provides the infrastructure for this relationship to be ongoing — not a transactional one-time event. The most effective clients view their Legacy team as a permanent part of their health infrastructure, not a service they use until the first protocol is complete.

Annual comprehensive biomarker panels (the full advanced panel discussed in Module 5) provide the data foundation for long-term protocol evolution. Use them as the benchmark for each new cycle.

---

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. Cycling appropriateness varies by compound: GH secretagogues (tesamorelin/ipamorelin) benefit from 4–8 week off-periods to maintain pituitary sensitivity; GLP-1 agonists can be used continuously for chronic metabolic conditions; repair peptides are used as needed after structural issues resolve.
2. Stopping GLP-1 agonists results in gradual return of appetite to pre-treatment levels — reinforcing why the nutrition habits built during the protocol are the durability mechanism. Stopping GH secretagogues returns you to an elevated baseline, not a starting point.
3. Long-term health optimization is a multi-cycle project. The clients who build health systems — with annual biomarker reviews, strategic protocol cycling, and ongoing Legacy medical team relationships — produce compounding outcomes over years.
